Moat Finder

A Buffett-style stock screener for the USA and Canada · In progress · Source on GitHub

Warren Buffett's approach to picking stocks is easy to say and hard to do at scale. He looks for businesses with a durable competitive advantage, a conservative balance sheet, pricing power, and predictable earnings, and he buys them only when the price leaves a margin of safety. Moat Finder applies those rules to every company in the S&P 500 and the S&P/TSX Composite. It scores each one from 0 to 100 and shows the top 10 in each sector for each country.

I wanted a screener that explains its picks instead of just ranking them. Each pick has a "See why" view that says in plain English which of Buffett's rules the company meets and which it misses. The view also shows up to ten years of financial history and how the estimated value was calculated.

What it does

  • Dashboard. Each pick is shown as a card with a score badge, the price next to the estimated value, a cheap/fair/expensive label, and key ratios. A sortable table view is also available.
  • Detail view. Shows the reasoning behind each score, the financial history, the valuation, and links to Yahoo Finance, Google Finance, and SEC filings.
  • Ask Moat Finder. A built-in chat assistant that runs on rules, with no AI model or API keys. It recognizes tickers and company names and handles questions like "Compare AAPL and MSFT," "Top 5 cheap tech stocks in the USA," "Low debt utilities," or "What is ROE?". It answers from the screen results.
  • Guide. Explains every term used in the app. The same glossary feeds the tooltips and the chat.
  • Light and dark themes. The theme follows your system setting, and a switch lets you override it.

How a stock is scored

PrincipleCriterionWeight
Durable advantageAverage ROE ≥ 15%, and ≥ 15% consistently12 + 8
Conservative balance sheetDebt/equity ≤ 0.5 (≤ 1.0 for utilities and real estate; ROA for financials)15
Pricing powerNet margin ≥ 15% (3-year average)14
Predictable earningsProfitable every year, growing, few down years6 + 5 + 4
Owner earningsPositive and growing free cash flow6 + 5
Margin of safetyPrice vs. DCF intrinsic value, P/E17 + 8

Intrinsic value comes from a 10-year discounted cash flow model. It starts from three-year average free cash flow (net income for financials). That figure grows at the lower of historical earnings growth and FCF growth, capped at 12%, then fades to 2.5% and is discounted at 10%. If data for a criterion is missing, the criterion is skipped and the other weights are rescaled. A stock needs at least three years of history and 60% data coverage to be ranked.

Where the data comes from

No single free source is complete or always right, so Moat Finder combines several. Prices and P/E ratios use the median across sources, and any disagreements between them appear as warnings on the stock.

SourceUsed for
Yahoo FinanceQuotes, ratios, about 4 years of statements
SEC EDGAR10+ years of official 10-K figures via the XBRL API (US)
Google FinancePrice and P/E cross-check
FinvizPrice, P/E, ROE, D/E, and margin cross-check (US)
Financial Modeling PrepFills gaps in history, mostly for Canada (optional)
WikipediaIndex constituents and GICS sectors

Responses are cached locally, with quotes kept for a day and fundamentals for a week. Requests are rate-limited so each site isn't overloaded. A full first run takes about 10 to 20 minutes, and later runs are fast. The screen can be refreshed from the dashboard or from the command line (python -m buffett), which makes it easy to schedule with cron.

Built with

Python, Streamlit, pandas, yfinance, SEC EDGAR XBRL, pytest.
